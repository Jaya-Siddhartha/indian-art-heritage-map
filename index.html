Colours of India

An interactive art heritage map

Indian art did not grow in one place. Painted caves, carved temples, court workshops and village walls are spread across the subcontinent, and each region developed its own materials, stories and styles. This atlas places fifteen art traditions on a real map of India so you can compare them by region.

      About

      About this project

      Objective

      This interactive map, made for an Indian Art History assignment, shows where major Indian art traditions are found. It places prehistoric rock art, Buddhist caves, temple sculpture, court painting, folk painting and crafts in their regions so the geographical variety of Indian art can be seen at a glance.

      Reading the map

      Every marker is one of three kinds of place: a monument site (a specific historic site such as Ajanta), a regional tradition (an art form practised across an area, such as Warli painting), or a city art centre (a city linked to a school or craft). Regional traditions are not limited to the marked point.
